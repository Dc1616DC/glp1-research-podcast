**Dan:** Well, take the cardiovascular benefits. Yes, we have some solid data from the SELECT trial and others showing cardiovascular risk reduction. **Alex:** Speaking of risks, let's talk about that case report – study three. This is about an elderly patient who had severe complications when restarting high-dose semaglutide after a break. What happened here?

**Dan:** This is exactly the kind of case that should make everyone pause. An elderly patient who had previously tolerated semaglutide went off it for a prolonged period, then restarted at their previous high dose. Within hours – not days or weeks – they developed severe gastrointestinal complications with systemic effects.

**Alex:** That seems unusual. I thought these medications were generally well-tolerated.

**Dan:** They usually are, but this highlights something really important about drug holidays. When patients stop these medications for extended periods, they essentially reset their tolerance. Their GI system is no longer adapted to the effects on gastric emptying and gut motility. Starting back at a high dose is like giving it to a naive patient, but potentially worse because of changes that occurred during the break.

**Alex:** So what's the practical takeaway for clinicians?

**Dan:** Simple – when patients restart after more than a few weeks off, you need to re-titrate from the beginning. I don't care if they were on 2.4mg for two years. Start low and go slow, just like with any new patient. The case report authors got this right – elderly patients with comorbidities are particularly vulnerable, but honestly, I'd apply this principle across age groups.

**Alex:** That makes sense. Now let's shift gears to something completely different – the dermatology review. This looked at skin-related side effects and potential benefits of GLP-1 agonists. Dan, I have to admit, I didn't even know these drugs could affect the skin.

Most reactions seem to be mild to moderate and reversible, but we're probably missing cases. **Alex:** Should dermatologists be prescribing these medications then?

**Dan:** Whoa, let's pump the brakes here. These are small studies with limited follow-up. We're nowhere near ready to add inflammatory skin diseases to the indication list. But it does suggest we should be monitoring skin changes more systematically in patients who are on these medications.

**Alex:** Fair enough. Our last study is actually about liraglutide specifically for obesity – kind of going back to basics. What did this review find?

**Dan:** This was an integrative review looking at off-label use of liraglutide for obesity over the last 10 years. **Alex:** The alcohol piece is interesting. Are you counseling patients about that?

**Alex:** Let's wrap up with key takeaways. Dan, what should clinicians be thinking about based on these studies?

**Dan:** First, patient education is critical, and you can't rely on social media to do it for you. Actively ask patients what they've been reading or watching online. Third, monitor for skin changes and don't automatically attribute new dermatologic symptoms to unrelated causes.

**Dan:** Be really skeptical of what you see on social media. If a video doesn't identify the credentials of the person speaking, that's a red flag.
